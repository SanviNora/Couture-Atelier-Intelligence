# Gaps and Decisions Log

Every data issue, modelling shortcut, and judgment call found while building the project. Each one is either fixed, flagged for a later phase, or accepted as a stated limitation. The interview questions column feeds the interview guide.

Status: **Fixed** (resolved in code, with a test where possible) · **Flagged** (kept and marked; a later phase decides) · **Accepted** (a stated limitation)

| ID | Area | Finding | Status | Where handled | Interview question it invites |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| G1 | Data | Visuelle weekly sales are net of returns: 3,445 returned units against 1.6M sold, almost all −1, clustered in weeks 4–5 and 9–10. Validation stopped the pipeline on first run. | Fixed | `ingest_visuelle.py` allows negatives down to −5 and stops on anything larger; `fct_store_product_week` splits `units_sold` and `units_returned`; tested | "Your pipeline failed on its first run. What did you do?" |
| G2 | Data | Returns cannot be linked to clients; client purchase records contain only purchases. Client spend is not net of returns. | Accepted | Returns are 0.2% of units, so the effect on client value is negligible | "How do returns affect your CLV numbers?" |
| G3 | Data | Client purchases reference 19 stores and 222 products that are not in the sales panel. | Flagged | `dim_store.in_sales_panel`, `dim_product.has_attributes` | "How did you handle records that don't join cleanly?" |
| G4 | Data | One store-product week has a missing discount value; reshaping silently dropped that week until nulls were kept. | Fixed | Reshape keeps nulls; `discount_missing` flag; discount treated as 0 | "How do you catch silent row loss in SQL?" |
| G5 | Data | Visuelle prices are max-normalised for confidentiality, so real euro prices are unknown. | Accepted | Price index mapped onto a €650–€9,500 ladder by percentile (`configs/calibration.yml`); all euro figures are labelled as modelled | "Where do your euro numbers come from?" |
| G6 | Data | 25 IDs are shared or promotional cards, not people. The top three made 18,737, 7,543 and 4,953 visits across 69 stores in about four days around Black Friday 2017 and New Year 2018. They inflated top-client concentration from 2.7% to 4.7%. | Fixed | Rule: an ID with 10+ receipts in one day or 4+ stores in one day is a shared card; excluded from client modelling, kept in store demand | "How did you find and handle outliers in client data?" |
| G7 | Leakage | The first luxury layer set each client's spend multiplier from their whole history, including future periods that phase 2 predicts. A model trained on those values would have seen the future. | Fixed | Replaced with a time-safe trade-up: each purchase is worth list value × (1 + min(prior visits, 12))^β, using only past visits; one global β | "Did you have any leakage, and how did you find it?" |
| G14 | Data | 36 IDs have 100+ visits, several a week for years: likely staff or personal-shopper accounts. They produced clients worth tens of millions of euros. | Fixed | Excluded from client modelling (`is_staff_like`), kept in store demand | "How do you tell a real VIC from a data artefact?" |
| G15 | Calibration | The first target (top 0.1% hold 23% of spend) describes all luxury consumers across the market, not one brand's clients. Forcing it, while keeping total spend fixed, shrank one-time buyers' purchases to about €60 a year. | Fixed | Target changed to the brand-level figure: VICs hold about 30% of a brand's revenue (top 1% assumed). First purchases keep their list price. BCG's market figure is kept as a cross-check | "Why did you change your calibration target?" |
| G16 | Calibration | The VIC target source is press coverage of an Altagamma conference, and the VIC share of clients (1%) is a project assumption. | Accepted | Labelled in `configs/calibration.yml` | "How reliable is your 30% figure?" |
| G17 | Calibration | Top-tier clients (top 0.1%) average about €896k a year. That clears BCG's €50k top-tier threshold but is high, because fast-fashion visit frequency is combined with luxury prices. | Accepted | Reported in `reports/luxury_layer.json`; used for ranking, not absolute claims | "Are your euro figures realistic?" |
| G18 | Modelling | BG/NBD's dropout parameter is below 1, so the textbook formula for expected visits is invalid. | Fixed | Exact computation by Gauss-Jacobi quadrature, tested against the textbook formula where valid | "What did you do when a standard formula didn't apply?" |
| G19 | Modelling | BG/NBD's "probability still active" ranks worse than random for returning next year (AUC 0.37), because every one-time buyer is treated as certainly active. | Fixed | A return classifier replaces it (AUC 0.71). A modified BG/NBD (MBG/NBD) would be the model-based extension | "What are the weaknesses of BG/NBD?" |
| G20 | Modelling | Gamma-Gamma assumes stable spend per visit; clients trade up here, so the probabilistic model under-predicts totals by 33%. The trade-up is itself a modelled assumption of the luxury layer. | Accepted | Documented in `docs/client_intelligence.md` | "When does Gamma-Gamma break?" |
| G21 | Modelling | The gradient-boosting spend model over-predicts totals by 36%: trained on 2017, when clients were new and trading up fast, applied to 2019, when they had matured. | Flagged | Used for ranking only; totals need recalibration before finance use | "Your model is biased. Would you still ship it?" |
| G22 | Modelling | For finding next year's VICs, last year's spend is a hard baseline (45% found, vs 44% for the model). | Accepted | Reported honestly; ML adds value for ranking and return prediction instead | "Did your model beat a simple baseline?" |
| G23 | Scope | COVID 2020: return rate fell from 37.5% to 10.6%; all models over-predicted spend by 280–690%; ranking fell from 0.42 to 0.24. | Accepted | Reported as a stress test | "How would you monitor and protect this model in production?" |
| G8 | Leakage | Home store (and so advisor assignment) uses the client's whole history. | Accepted | Used only for operational assignment, not as a model feature | "Is anything in your features computed with future data?" |
| G9 | Scope | Visuelle is one Italian fast-fashion retailer (2016–2021), not a luxury house. | Accepted | Luxury behaviour comes from the calibrated layer, with every target cited; stated in the data card | "Why should a luxury house trust results from fast-fashion data?" |
| G10 | Scope | 2020 is distorted by COVID store closures. | Flagged | Phase 2 evaluates on 2018 and 2019, and reports 2020 separately as a stress test | "How would your model behave under a shock?" |
| G11 | Scope | Google Trends data covers Visuelle's categories, colours and fabrics, not real luxury products. | Accepted | Cut to fit the 1–2 week plan; possible extension | "What external signals would you add?" |
| G12 | Assumption | Consent is modelled as independent of behaviour, at a fixed opt-in rate, to avoid leaking future visits into consent. | Accepted | `configs/calibration.yml` | "Why not model consent from engagement?" |
| G13 | Retired | The original UCI data was UK giftware with many wholesale buyers. | Fixed | Replaced by Visuelle 2.0 | "Why did you change datasets mid-project?" |
| G24 | Reproducibility | Two runs with fixed seeds gave slightly different metrics (Spearman 0.426 vs 0.429): DuckDB returns grouped rows in no fixed order, which changed the order of the models' training data. | Fixed | Rows sorted by client before training; a full rebuild from raw files now reproduces the reports byte for byte | "How do you make an ML pipeline reproducible?" |
| G25 | Scope | Google Trends covers Oct 2021 to Oct 2026; Visuelle covers 2016 to 2021. The series cannot be model features. | Accepted | Used as a current market signal for the tier dial and dashboard; Visuelle's own bundled trends serve forecasting | "Why not use Google Trends in your models?" |
| G26 | Data | In the premium-brands download the shared anchor (LV Speedy) is reported as "<1" in 115 of 262 weeks, so rescaling it onto the common scale would be mostly rounding noise. | Fixed | An anchor is used only if under 10% of its weeks are "<1"; premium brands are compared within their own file | "How do you combine Google Trends downloads?" |
| G27 | Data | Percent change from a near-zero base is meaningless ("pre-owned luxury" would show +15,550%). | Fixed | No percent change when the first-year mean is below 1; levels are reported instead | "How do you avoid misleading growth numbers?" |
| G28 | Data | Google Trends is relative search interest from a manual download, mostly United States; it is not sales. "J.Crew" is flat near 1, likely because the search term is ambiguous. | Accepted | Checksums recorded in the download manifest; labelled as search interest everywhere | "Is search interest a good proxy for demand?" |
