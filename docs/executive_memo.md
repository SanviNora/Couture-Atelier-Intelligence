# Memo: four decisions for a luxury house, 2027 season

**To:** Client, merchandising and pricing leadership · **From:** Couture Atelier Intelligence · **Basis:** real behaviour of 665,000 clients and 110 boutiques (Visuelle 2.0, 2016–2021) with a luxury layer calibrated to published figures. Every figure is in `reports/`; every assumption in `configs/`.

## 1. Ladder down; do not cut list prices

A 10% cut needs 13% more volume just to break even. Real markdowns show that once the novelty fades (weeks three and four), demand rises about 5%. The cut would cost about **€48M** on an €806M range. An entry line instead (5% more units, a quarter cannibalising core pieces) adds about **€30M over two years**: clients whose first piece is entry-priced come back nearly as often as those who start higher (1.1 points less, same boutique and month). **Hold the icons; add pieces beneath them.**

## 2. Stop discounting, but keep a way out for unsold stock

Dropping every markdown changes profit by only **−0.5%**: markdown lift fades within weeks. The real cost of luxury discipline is having nowhere to send what does not sell. Refusing every exit costs about **€58M (10%)**. With the EU destruction ban in force since July 2026, a pre-owned, archive or recycling route is required anyway. **Fund the exit route before removing the outlet.**

## 3. Allocate scarce launches by forecast, and hero pieces by relationship

Splitting launches across boutiques by forecast earns **€32M** more than the current opening split when supply covers 60% of demand. For hero pieces the boutique split matters little; which clients are offered them matters a great deal. An integer programme that values the relationship (and reserves 30% for rising clients) earns **€67M vs €44M** for "VICs first". Serving every VIC costs about 8% of this season's revenue. **Treat that as an investment, not a leak.**

## 4. Keep advisors calling their best clients; test the model alongside

Uplift models chose different clients from one pilot to the next. On average they reached 83% of the best achievable outcome against 87% for calling the best clients. **Keep the current practice as the champion.** Run the model as a randomised challenger, read on visit rate, with a 5% client holdout (or 17% of advisors). Replace the champion only if the challenger wins live.

## What to watch

The return rate fell every year in this data (51% → 37.5%). That alone pushed next-year spend forecasts 47% too high, while the client rankings stayed sound. **Monitor outcomes weekly, not only model inputs.** Input drift flagged the 2019 shift but gave no warning before COVID.

*Limits: the price response is measured at a fashion retailer, not a luxury house; luxury demand is likely less price-sensitive, which makes cutting look worse still. Contact effects and margins are assumptions, each shown with a sensitivity range on the dashboard.*
